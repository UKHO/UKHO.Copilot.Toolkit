# Research brief

**Answer:** RPIR's principal delivery problem is not a lack of instructions to investigate: it is that those instructions are overridden by narrow repair authority, blanket stop/no-retry rules, compulsory coordination, and tests that enforce the restrictions. **Replace these rules with a delivery-first diagnose–repair–rerun–review loop**, not another layer of exceptions. An approved outcome should authorize ordinary implementation judgment and recovery within its change boundary. Ask the engineer promptly when a real decision or unavailable capability prevents further progress.

## Question, scope, and success

- **Request:** Research why the eight historical topic folders in `docs/planning/`, including their multiple implementation passes, did not demonstrate successful initial-plan completion, and identify changes needed to make RPIR deliver change.
- **Engineer direction:** Ordinary source-controlled changes are recoverable; effectiveness takes priority over overstated safeguards. Agents must resolve their own issues, or ask how to proceed instead of ending with an incomplete plan. Explicit correction: “We need to get rid of extensive stop conditions and no-retry rules.”
- **Scope:** Historical plans, reports, Reviews and all three README variants; active RPIR instructions, agents, skills, templates, validation scripts and workflow; official Microsoft documentation on feasible agent behavior. Research recommendations only, not an implementation plan, source edits, release, or runtime activation.
- **Success criteria:** Ordinary local failures and agent-introduced defects are repaired and retested in the same approved delivery effort; unmet original acceptance criteria found by Review can be corrected without unnecessary replanning; material decisions are raised promptly; unavailable tools produce a practical fallback or a specific question; reports never substitute for the promised deliverable.
- **Lifecycle identity:** Delivery-first RPIR workflow; new lifecycle, distinct from the historical remediation topics. Working revision R2, 2026-10-08; duplicate-draft correction, recommendations unchanged.
- **Evidence still limited:** Historical records are reports, not complete execution traces. Loaded customization versions and actual conversational behavior have not been runtime-tested. The conflicting topic 005/006 execution accounts remain unresolved. None of these limits prevents an evidence-based recommendation.

## Existing patterns and reusable components

- Preserve Research evidence informing an approved Plan, truthful acceptance criteria and independent review; remove unnecessary ceremony around ordinary correction.
- Reuse the existing question-resolution distinction between factual diagnosis and engineer-owned decisions, but remove the requirement that every safe repair have been predicted explicitly.
- Retain focused existing verification scripts and independent result groups. Topic 007 already improved exact-case discovery; current parity PASS is counterevidence to treating earlier discovery failures as unresolved today.
- Existing rules already waive routine Runner/ACL/global-scan prerequisites for ordinary Markdown saves. Apply that simplification consistently and remove remaining repeated allocation/status ceremonies rather than reintroducing probes.
- Reuse actual local diffs/preimages for targeted recovery and preserve unrelated engineer work. Do not require routine commits, full-workspace hashing or an additional backup ritual for every edit.
- Keep traceability and honest failed/unavailable/not-run distinctions; compress their representation rather than remove evidence of actual work.

## Assumptions

- “Get the job done” means satisfy the agreed outcome, not silently broaden requirements, weaken tests or mark incomplete work complete.
- Initial approval of consequential change remains useful. The request supports autonomous repair within that outcome; it does not require global autoapproval or automatic progression through every phase.
- Most ordinary code, customization and documentation edits can be corrected locally. Recovery uses available history/preimages without assuming all uncommitted, untracked or ignored contents are in Git.
- No new dependency, external service or technology is required merely to simplify these authored policies. Actual platform capability must be checked when implementing the chosen agent configuration.

## Options and trade-offs

1. **Add another exception to existing stop rules.** Evidence: topics 004, 006 and 007 repeatedly attempted narrow recovery improvements. Low initial diff, but preserves contradictory defaults and encourages further exception cycles. **Not recommended.**
2. **Replace the restrictive operating model and reconcile its owners/tests.** Outcome-based authority, ordinary repair/retry by default, independent review with an in-scope correction loop, concise records and material-only questions. Broader coordinated change, but addresses the observed mechanism rather than its wording. **Recommended.**
3. **Remove all controls or enable unrestricted autoapproval.** Not necessary for productive recovery and not requested. Git cannot undo external publication, secret disclosure or lost unrecorded work. **Not recommended.**

## Risks and constraints

- Do not replace extensive no-retry clauses with an equally extensive catalogue of retry prerequisites. Use one short principle with practical examples; reserve additional checks for evidenced risk.
- A retry must be productive: inspect the failure, correct the implementation/invocation or test a different supported hypothesis. Repeating an unchanged failing operation indefinitely is not delivery. When no useful next action remains, explain the attempts and ask the engineer how to proceed; do not impose an arbitrary universal retry quota.
- In-scope correction must preserve agreed behavior and acceptance criteria. New features, materially different public behavior, consequential dependencies or security/compatibility trade-offs need a decision, not guessed approval.
- Preserve unrelated user work. Targeted restoration of an agent's own accidental edit is different from destructive resets or indiscriminate cleanup.
- Actual platform denial, secrets and uncertain destructive/external operations remain meaningful boundaries. A local failing build is not inherently equivalent to a partially completed deployment. Tests must be understood by their effects, not assumed harmless solely from their name.
- Current packaging and runtime-activation holds remain respected during this Research. They are not evidence that all such repository-authored ceremony must be retained in a future design. No package was built and neither ignored archive was changed.
- Static text validation cannot prove agents finish tasks. Runtime demonstrations remain unavailable until separately authorized. No historical trace reconstruction or extra permission probe is needed merely to publish these findings.

If a material decision or unavailable capability prevents progress, ask the engineer promptly with the evidence and a recommended next action, while continuing unaffected work. A partial report is a checkpoint, not successful delivery.**
