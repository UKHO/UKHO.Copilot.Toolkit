## Recommendation and validation

1. **Reconcile acceptance and scope, not the whole historical narrative.** Use P1 and V1 as evidence, the current committed source as the baseline, and this brief for the additional gaps. Resolve V1-7's intended disposition without destructive rollback. 2. **Close the proven holes in the existing checks.** Fix complete handoff parsing, CI route coverage, delegation boundaries, additive contradictions, real malformed input and consumed lineage fixtures. Keep the dependency-free Node/CommonJS approach unless an engineer-approved dependency decision becomes necessary. Preserve meaningful positives and negative attribution; a failing required check remains unmet while diagnosis continues.
3. **Reconcile remaining policy and reader contradictions.** Correct the narrow owners above, preserving the delivery-first model, material decisions, real denial, user-work preservation and independent Review. Do not append another large exception catalogue or reintroduce mandatory Runner use.
4. **Revalidate and obtain fresh independent Review.** Run all three existing static surfaces after correction, inspect fixture restoration/effects, and assess original acceptance rather than exit codes alone. Seek pinned Node 22.14.0 evidence for the malformed-input compatibility investigation through a permitted available environment; report unavailable evidence without inventing a new hosted-CI or dual-environment gate. Do not silently install or trigger remote effects. Preserve prior records; new implementation evidence and a clean independent Review, not an optional status field, establish completion of the bounded authored/static deliverable.
5. **Keep runtime effectiveness as a separate evidence track.** After separate activation authorization, exercise actual diagnosis → repair → rerun → Review correction. Until then, say authored/static completion only. Packaging is not a substitute for runtime proof and is not part of this completion request.

**Options:** Accepting the present green checks is least effort but contradicts the known findings. Another broad RPIR redesign repeats already delivered work and risks more policy drift. Targeted completion is recommended: more focused tests and a small number of owner corrections, at the cost of exposing formerly missed failures. Do not turn these Research recommendations into a rigid exhaustive repair whitelist.
